Social Media Listening
Information Architecture + UX

Listen to every platform at once: trends, brands, topics and the creators who move them

The platform analyses social media posts across multiple platforms to uncover emerging trends, brand performance, topic engagement, and influencer activity. Designed for Social Media Marketing Managers and Market Research Analysts, it enables users to benchmark their brand against competitors, compare the performance of different topics, and identify influential voices within relevant conversations.

problem statement

Conversations about a brand, a product category or a competitor happen everywhere at once.

On X, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit and LinkedIn. Each platform has its own format, its own audience and its own way of measuring engagement, so the full picture is never in one place.

Marketing managers and research analysts end up stitching exports, spreadsheets and screenshots together by hand. By the time a report is ready, the trend has moved on. It is hard to tell which topics are growing, how the brand compares with competitors, which creators are genuinely relevant rather than simply large, and which complaints or buying signals need a reply today. The result is slower decisions, campaign budgets spent on guesswork, and missed chances to act while a conversation is still live.

who it's for

Two primary users who read the same conversation for different reasons.

Murmur is designed around two primary users who read the same conversation for different reasons:

persona 01

Social Media Marketing Manager

“I need to know what to post, where, when — and who should post it with us.”

Goals
  • Grow brand awareness and share of voice
  • Pick creators who fit a product or campaign
  • Spend the campaign budget where it lands
  • Answer complaints and questions before they escalate
persona 02

Market Research Analyst

“I need to see what people really think, how it is changing, and how we compare.”

Goals
  • Spot emerging trends and rising topics early
  • Track sentiment by topic and by platform
  • Benchmark the brand against competitors
  • Surface unmet needs that point to product innovation

information architecture

Everything starts from one question: what is being said about a keyword?

A single filter bar — keyword, time range (4 weeks, 12 weeks or 6 months) and source — sits at the top and applies to every screen, so users never lose their context when they move between views. The content is split into six tabs, each answering one job.

01

Overview

The big picture and what changed: mentions, mood, brand share and auto-surfaced discoveries.

02

Topics

Themes inside the conversation, how people feel about each one, and where it is discussed.

03

Brands

Your share of voice against competitors, platform by platform.

04

Creators

Find and compare the voices that matter, including small creators with high relevance.

05

Campaigns

Where, when and what to promote next, with creators who already fit the topic.

06

Inbox

Customer care and new leads that need a reply, sorted by priority.

the solution
Screen 01 · Overview

The big picture, and what changed.

The landing screen answers "how are we doing?" in seconds. Total mentions with the change on the previous period, the number of creators in the conversation, and an overall mood scale sit at the top. Below, brand share of voice, mentions over time split by sentiment, top posts filtered by tone and trending topics give the detail.

Discoveries are surfaced automatically — the fastest-growing topic, negative talk to watch, hidden-gem creators and competitors gaining share — each with a link straight to the screen that explains it.

Screen 02 · Topics

Themes inside the conversation.

Posts are grouped into themes that can be sorted by most talked about, fastest growing or most negative. Selecting a topic shows its trend, how people feel, which platforms it lives on, example posts and the creators driving it.

“every sunscreen leaves me grey” becomes “a tinted SPF for deeper skin tones”

Insights turn complaints into product ideas — with the number of mentions and growth behind each one, giving product teams evidence for innovation.

Screen 03 · Brands

Benchmark against competitors.

A one-line summary states the brand's position in plain language — its share of brand mentions and the platform where it leads. Share of voice is broken down by platform, so it is clear where competitors are winning, and a scorecard compares mentions, share, change and sentiment for every brand side by side.

Screen 04 · Creators

The right voices, not just the loudest.

Creators are ranked by how much of what they post is on-topic, alongside followers, engagement, posting frequency and tone. A "hidden gems" filter surfaces smaller accounts with high relevance and strong engagement — often a better fit for a product launch than a large, general audience. Up to three creators can be compared side by side before a brief goes out.

Screen 05 · Campaigns

Where, when and what to promote next.

Listening turns into a plan. A recommended next campaign names the topic, platform, day and time in one sentence, with a button to brief the best-fitting creator. A heatmap shows positive engagement by topic and platform, another shows the best time to post by day and daypart, and "messages that land" reuses the language of the most positive posts — supporting targeted strategies and better budget allocation.

Screen 06 · Inbox

Customer care and new leads.

Posts that need a reply are collected in one place and split into two queues: customer care (complaints, questions and feedback that mention the brand) and leads (people asking for recommendations, comparing brands or ready to buy).

Each post is prioritised by urgency, age and reach, so the oldest urgent posts come first, and comes with a suggested reply that can be drafted and marked as done.

design decisions
Design decision 01

One filter bar for every screen

Keyword, time and source sit above the tabs because they apply to all of them. Switching screens never resets what the user is looking at.

Design decision 02

Insights that lead somewhere

Every discovery ends in an action — explore topic, read posts, compare creator, compare brands — that opens the screen with the right item already selected.

Design decision 03

Plain-language summaries

Key screens open with a sentence, not a chart: "You hold 16.5% of brand mentions and lead on YouTube." Charts then back the sentence up.

Design decision 04

A calm, focused visual system

Off-white tonal layers, ink-black selection and a single coral highlight, set in Inter Tight with pill shapes. Colour is reserved for data and the one thing that needs attention.

Design decision 05

Themes separate from components

Built on the NucliOS design system. The Murmur look is a theme layered on top, so colours can change without touching a single component, and the product supports dark mode.

Design decision 06

Shareable, accessible views

The open tab lives in the URL, so a refresh or a shared link reopens the same screen. A skip link, labelled controls and a fully responsive layout keep it usable for everyone.
